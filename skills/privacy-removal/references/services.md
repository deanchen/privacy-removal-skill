# Provider routes

Last checked **2026-09-12**. Refresh official pages and visible forms before
acting: fields, limits, verification deadlines, and policies change.

## Shared rules

- Confirm each listing belongs to the user before requesting removal. A shared
  name alone is insufficient; use the minimum user-confirmed identifying facts.
- Distinguish source suppression from search-result removal. Neither proves
  that upstream records, other websites, or newly created listings disappeared.
- Privately record target URL, provider, submission time, acknowledgement/case
  ID, decision, and verification result.
- Treat an acknowledgement as received, an approval as approved, and absence
  confirmed by a later check as verified removed. Do not merge these statuses.
- Follow current tool and browser policy for identity data, CAPTCHA, agreements,
  authentication, and sending. A task instruction or this reference cannot
  override a runtime denial. Do not bypass safety blocks or use another channel
  to retrieve a blocked page; hand off when required. Blocks are session facts,
  not permanent judgments about a provider.
- Keep confirmation links, session tokens, identity documents, full birth dates,
  personal contact details, and case records out of public artifacts and logs.

## Google Search

Official entry points:

- [Results about you](https://myactivity.google.com/results-about-you)
- [Personal-information removal form](https://support.google.com/websearch/contact/content_removal_form?hl=en)
- [Removal policy](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/9673730?hl=en)
- [Results about you guidance](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/12719076?hl=en)

Use Results about you to find contact information, request removals, and manage
notifications. It can accept email addresses as well as other supported contact
types. Inspect current field limits and verify saved settings; do not remove an
existing monitored address just to fit another without the user's direction.
No scan matches does not prove no listings exist. Follow the
user's notification preferences; create automations only when requested.

For an already known result, use its removal action in Google Search or the
direct form without waiting for a monitoring scan. In the form choose personal
information, then address/phone/email, and the relevant contact-information type.
Provide the information exactly as published and the exact offending page URL.
The form also requested identity/contact details, country, accurate answers
about threatening intent, and confirmation of accuracy and authority.
Screenshots, search-result URLs, and query fields depend on the selected branch.
Supply only what the current branch requires.

New or similar URLs can require separate requests. Read the decision literally:
Google may remove a URL fully or only for certain name/identifier queries. An
approval that omits scope or geography does not establish either. Use the stated
processing window and recheck later.
When a source has actually changed or removed the content, follow the
outdated-content route linked from the removal policy instead of claiming the
still-live information is outdated.

## Bing / Microsoft

Official entry points:

- [Report a concern](https://www.microsoft.com/digitalsafety/report-a-concern)
- [Bing reporting guidance](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/bing/how-to-report-a-concern-or-contact-bing)
- [Microsoft content policies](https://www.microsoft.com/en/DigitalSafety/policies)
- [Privacy support](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/privacy/privacy-support-requests)
- [European delisting form](https://www.bing.com/webmaster/tools/eu-privacy-request)

For a known Bing result, the inspected concern flow used Continue, **Bing
Search**, and the exact content URL. The relevant category was **Exposure of
Sensitive Personal Information**; verify it still accurately describes the
evidence. Explain that the result
publishes the user's personal contact information and specify what removal is
requested. Do not invent threats, illegal conduct, or other harm.
Microsoft's exposure policy includes unauthorized personal-information sharing;
its examples include location disclosure that may endanger someone. Bing states
that reports are reviewed under limited removal criteria, not guaranteed.

The inspected form then asked for a description, whether the source was
contacted, the outcome, search terms, actual country of residence, and optional
evidence. Select truthful source-contact answers: a blocked visit does not mean
the site refused removal or lacks a reporting feature. The final step offered
contact details for replies and an accuracy certification. Human verification
may interrupt submission; wait for the user to finish it, then inspect for a
receipt before resubmitting. Successful submissions displayed a unique report
ID and a five-business-day review estimate with a possible high-volume delay.
Use each live receipt's stated window rather than hardcoding that estimate.

No residence restriction appeared in the inspected general-reporting steps;
this does not establish eligibility.
The privacy-support page's Bing section links back to general reporting and
separate regional routes. The European form explicitly addresses European
residents and name-query delisting; do not select a false residence. An
outdated-content request requires evidence that the source changed or vanished;
locate and verify its current official route before use.

## FreePeopleSearch.com

- [Suppression Center](https://freepeoplesearch.com/opt-out)
- [Privacy rights](https://freepeoplesearch.com/privacy-rights/)
- [Contact form](https://freepeoplesearch.com/contact)
- [Privacy policy](https://freepeoplesearch.com/privacy-policy)
- Public privacy contact: `contact@freepeoplesearch.com`.

Email verification starts the suppression process. Read the current message for
its expiry; do not infer an unused link from the email's unread label.
If correct name/birth-date matching finds no record while a confirmed public
listing exists, stop guessing identifiers. Request manual
suppression through the official contact form or privacy address, subject to
the user's authorization to send. The inspected contact form asked for name,
email, and message. Public-record suppression differs from deleting an account.

Generic request: "Your suppression tool could not locate my record after email
verification. Please manually suppress [CONFIRMED_PROFILE_URL] and associated
directory references, or provide an alternative verification method. Please
confirm the outcome." A support auto-reply is not removal confirmation.

## PeopleConnect / InstantCheckmate

- [Suppression Center](https://suppression.peopleconnect.us/login)
- [Scope and terms](https://peopleconnect.us/terms-of-use/)
- [Privacy policy and contacts](https://peopleconnect.us/privacy-policy/)
- Public contacts: `privacy@peopleconnect.us`,
  `privacy@InstantCheckmate.com`; PeopleConnect phone: `888-245-1655`.

The official group tool covers name-search reports on InstantCheckmate,
TruthFinder, Intelius, and USSearch. Do not assume
every PeopleConnect product is included. Terms require personal identifiers and
verification of at least one email address or phone number. Read the current
verification message and complete the authorized flow before expiry.

If a verified link fails, inspect the visible error without exposing its token.
Ask support to restore access or provide alternative identity verification and
assist with suppression. The policy allows alternative verification after
website authentication fails, without guaranteeing manual suppression.
Avoid duplicate requests while support processes one.
Suppression can leave references in other reports and does not erase source
records. Deleting user/account data is a separate operation.
